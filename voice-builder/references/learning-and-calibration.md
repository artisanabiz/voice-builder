# Learn expression and development

Read how meaning is expressed, not only punctuation, sentence length, or memorable phrases. Notice:

- Vocabulary and phrase constructions, register, natural borrowing and language mixing.
- Sentence construction and variation, paragraph development, transitions, emphasis, and uncertainty.
- Reader relationship: how the narrator explains, asks, disagrees, qualifies, or addresses the reader.
- Information selection: what gets explained, what background is supplied, how examples and quotations earn their place.
- Development: chronology, attributed motives, mechanisms, comparisons, consequences, changes of perspective, digressions and returns.
- Stable recurring choices, including opening or closing signatures, distinguished from occasional expressions.

Keep lightweight working observations with relevant evidence and exceptions. A handful of varied examples can support a starting point, not a universal voice claim. There is no required count before trying useful writing. User agreement to try a trait is a preference or provisional decision, not evidence that every original text has that trait.

## Compare functions across samples

An author may explain one event directly and develop another through a long anecdote. Different structures can express the same voice. Ask what a feature does in context: does a question open an uncertainty that the text answers, a comparison make a mechanism understandable, a digression supply relevant context, a repeated ending establish a reader relationship?

Distinguish a distinctive signature with a stable role from a phrase that happens to occur in one sample. Propose exact retention as an option alongside adaptation or omission. Avoid silently deleting a signature because it repeats, or forcing every recognizable expression into every draft.

Some reference writing is openly evaluative. Preserve the user's chosen editorial stance while testing which expression habits transfer. A request for neutral reporting does not require impersonal prose, but an author's beliefs, allegiances, experiences, and certainty do not become reusable facts or preferences of the user.

## Propose guidance that changes writing

Separate three layers in the proposal:

1. Observed habits, bounded to the supplied material.
2. User choices, such as more irony or an exact closing.
3. Adaptations to test, such as using a voice learned from messages in an article.

Use concrete guidance about expression and development. Broad labels such as natural, warm, or journalistic can summarize a direction but cannot replace it. For an unfamiliar language or format, explain that the adaptation is untested; do not reject useful samples solely for being in another format.

Do not turn absent features into prohibitions, infer a whole personality, or convert accidental typos into mandatory imperfections. Reproduce natural usage without inventing personal experiences or weakening factual precision to sound human.

## Learn from what fails

Evaluate fluency, voice, factual fidelity, and the requested angle separately. Ask only for the useful next distinction. Present neutral, concrete choices; do not recommend accepting a draft or suggest that the user should like it. Accept direct edits without another quiz.

If the text is fluent but generic, examine both its language and its information selection/development. If storytelling feels forced, compare specific questions, transitions, reveals, or conclusions. Do not respond by banning all questions or all storytelling: the next sample may use them meaningfully. Correct the function or wording demonstrated to fail.

When useful, compare a short passage, a user's edit, or a reference account of the same event. A reference answer may reveal missing information as well as differences in voice; separate these explanations. Do not copy additional claims into the new draft without verifying them. Once that answer has been consulted, classify the topic as calibration material and choose a different topic for a later independent trial.

Keep the sequence small: locate the mismatch, propose a bounded change, try the relevant passage, and revise the durable guidance if the user confirms it. Avoid making the person judge endless complete rewrites with the same vague choices. If a correction could be local or lasting, ask about its scope; explicit lasting instructions already settle this.

Generated drafts are not additional original samples. An accepted draft can support calibration, but approval of writing does not automatically authorize exporting it as an exact example. No AI-detector score, adjective list, or structural validator proves faithful voice transfer.
